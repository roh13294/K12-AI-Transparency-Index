2026 National K–12 AI Transparency Index
Executive Summary
In 2026, an independent review was conducted of 353 public school districts across six U.S. states: Michigan, California, Texas, Florida, New York, and Illinois. The purpose of this review was to assess whether districts publicly disclose governance practices related to artificial intelligence use in K–12 settings.
Using a standardized 100-point transparency framework, districts were evaluated based solely on publicly accessible information available on official district websites. The index measures the presence of:
Publicly accessible AI policy documentation


Disclosure of AI tool usage


Named oversight authority


Board-level references to AI governance


Public contact pathways for AI-related oversight


Key Findings
96% of districts demonstrate either minimal transparency or no publicly accessible AI governance signals.


23% of districts show no identifiable public AI governance signals.


73% demonstrate limited transparency.


4% show emerging governance practices.


0% of districts in the sample met the threshold for leading transparency.


The findings do not imply districts are or are not using AI internally. Rather, they reflect the current state of public-facing governance transparency.
As AI tools increasingly influence curriculum development, student services, administrative workflows, and educational decision-making, the absence of visible governance structures raises important questions about public accountability and stakeholder awareness.
The 2026 Index establishes a baseline measurement. Districts may request review updates if new public documentation is published.
