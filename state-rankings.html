How to Interpret These Rankings

Use the rankings as a disclosure baseline, not a verdict

Public transparency signals only

These rankings are based on public-facing transparency signals located under the project methodology, not hidden internal practice.

What a low score means

A low score means limited public-facing documentation was found under the methodology. It does not prove the absence of internal policy.

What a high score means

A higher score means stronger public evidence of transparency, governance language, oversight, or public disclosure in the audited materials.

What the rankings should prompt

The goal is review and improvement, not blame. Rankings should help districts, lawmakers, and families ask clearer questions about disclosure.

Using the Data Responsibly

The index should be used to ask better questions, not to assume hidden misconduct. A low score means the project did not find clear public-facing evidence under the methodology. Districts may submit corrections or public links for review.
