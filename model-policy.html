Model District AI Transparency Policy

A practical starting point for public AI disclosure, student data protections, and human review.

This model policy gives districts a practical starting point for public AI disclosure, student data protections, and human review. It reflects a simple principle: We are not anti-AI. We are pro-transparency. It is designed to help schools use AI responsibly while keeping students and families informed.

1. Public AI Tool Registry

Districts should publish a public list of AI-enabled tools used in instructional, administrative, counseling, monitoring, disciplinary, assessment, or student support contexts.

2. Notice for High-Impact Uses

Students and families should be notified when AI tools are used in grading, discipline, counseling, academic placement, attendance intervention, monitoring, plagiarism detection, special education support, or other high-impact contexts.

3. Human Review

AI should not be the sole basis for serious academic, disciplinary, placement, counseling, or educational record consequences.

4. Appeal Process

Students and families should be able to request human review, explanation, correction of inaccurate data, and reconsideration of AI-influenced decisions.

5. Student Data Protection

Student data processed by AI-enabled tools should not be sold, used for unrelated advertising, or used for secondary model training unless explicitly permitted by law and district policy.

6. Annual Transparency Report

Districts should publish an annual summary of AI tools used, purposes, high-impact use cases, complaints or appeals, policy updates, and vendor changes.

How a District Can Start

Use this as a starting point for a school board discussion.

  • Publish a simple AI disclosure page.
  • List AI-enabled tools currently used.
  • Identify whether student data is processed.
  • State where human review is required.
  • Provide a parent or student contact for questions.
  • Review vendor contracts for AI and data terms.
  • Update the page annually.
